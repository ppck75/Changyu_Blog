# AI Competition Is Not Merely Technological or Economic but Also Ideological

Global competition in artificial intelligence is often understood as a race for technological superiority and economic advantage. Countries compete to develop more powerful AI models, secure advanced semiconductors, and dominate emerging markets. However, I think AI competition should also be understood as an **ideological struggle over how technology ought to shape society.**

I recently read *Framing and Legitimating Artificial Intelligence in China’s English-Language State Media, 2014–2024* by Pham and Liu (2026), which prompted me to reflect on this broader dimension of AI competition.

## Beyond Technological Superiority

AI development is not politically or ideologically neutral. Behind technological advancement lie fundamental questions about the relationship between the state, the market, and society.

Who should govern AI? What values should guide its development? Should technological innovation prioritize economic growth, individual freedom, national security, or collective well-being?

Different answers to these questions reflect different political values and visions of society. In this sense, competition over AI is also competition over the kind of future that technology should help create.

## From Technological Leadership to Normative Leadership

The study by Pham and Liu provides an interesting illustration of this phenomenon.

Following the emergence of ChatGPT, Chinese state media temporarily placed less emphasis on domestic technological achievements while increasingly highlighting international cooperation, global AI governance, and normative leadership.

This shift suggests that international AI competition involves not only demonstrating technological superiority but also establishing the legitimacy of a particular governance model.

When technological leadership becomes contested, the ability to define international norms and principles can become another source of political influence.

## Who Defines the Future of AI?

The significance of global AI competition, therefore, extends beyond which country develops the most advanced technology.

**It also concerns whose values become embedded in AI systems, whose governance principles become international standards, and who possesses the authority to shape the future of AI.**

Perhaps the most consequential question is not simply who will win the AI race, but who will define what winning means.
