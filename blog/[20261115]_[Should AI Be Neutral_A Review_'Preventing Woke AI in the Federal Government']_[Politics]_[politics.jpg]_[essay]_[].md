# Should AI Be Neutral_A Review_'Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government'

Executive Order: [Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/preventing-woke-ai-in-the-federal-government/)

Issued: July 23, 2025

Executive Order in Three Points

1. The order argues that efforts to promote diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) in AI can introduce ideological bias and distort factual accuracy.

2. It establishes two principles for AI models procured by the federal government: Truth-seeking and Ideological Neutrality.

3. It requires federal agencies to incorporate these principles into their AI procurement processes and contracts.

## Some Thoughts After Reading
These are just some thoughts that came to mind while reading the Executive Order. Rather than a structured analysis or a well-developed argument, this is more of a personal reflection—an attempt to put my initial reactions, questions, and uncertainties into words.

I have generally understood AI bias as discrimination against minority or socially vulnerable groups. However, just as reverse discrimination can occur, excessively prioritizing the perspectives of certain groups may also undermine fairness. It is true that AI can reproduce the perspectives of dominant or majority groups, as these perspectives are often more prevalent in its training data. Interestingly, however, this Executive Order raises a different concern: efforts to make AI unbiased may themselves introduce new forms of bias.

This leads me to several questions. **Is it really appropriate to pursue AI neutrality through deliberate engineering? And is genuine neutrality even possible?** After all, the real world itself is not neutral.

Journalism offers an interesting parallel. Complete neutrality in news reporting is nearly impossible. Moreover, some people says that simply presenting facts without providing context or critical interpretation does not necessarily fulfill journalism's broader role in society.

Ideally, AI should be able to present different perspectives at the same time, such as arguments for and against an issue or liberal and conservative viewpoints. However, AI learns from data produced by human societies, which are inherently shaped by social values, power relations, and existing biases. Therefore, the training data itself cannot be assumed to be neutral.

**As a media studies student, I think it is worth examining AI through the lens of journalism and its social functions. This raises two fundamental questions: Should AI be neutral in the first place? And even if neutrality is desirable, is it actually achievable?**

I found myself thinking about the relationship between AI neutrality and the role of journalism. I believe that applying the insights gained from journalism's long-standing debates to AI could also help shape the future direction of AI development. I'm not trying to reach a definite conclusion here, but simply exploring a few questions that I think are worth considering.
